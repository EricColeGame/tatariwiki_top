## Why Your Tatari Upgrade Priority Decides the Run

Most players don't lose to tatari because their numbers are too low. They lose because their first big investment went into the wrong stat, and by the time the curse stacks past its threshold, there's no way back. A well-planned tatari upgrade priority is the difference between fighting the mechanic and farming it.

Tatari comes from Japanese folklore, where it describes a curse or vengeful influence — the lingering grudge of someone wronged, or a punishment visited on a community that neglected its obligations. Games borrow the idea almost literally: tatari usually works as a stacking affliction that builds when you take certain actions and escalates into penalties. Because it's a threshold mechanic, your tatari upgrade priority matters more than any single flat stat gain.

Folklore traditions around appeasing angry spirits were a serious civic matter in premodern Japan, and the logic carries over into how these systems are designed today — see this overview of [Japanese mythology and spirit appeasement rituals](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Japanese-mythology) for background. The design lesson is simple: you don't beat a curse by hitting harder. You beat it by controlling when it triggers and what it costs you.

## What Each Upgrade Category Actually Buys You

Before ranking anything, separate the upgrade list into categories. Almost every tatari progression tree mixes five or six of them, and they are not remotely equal in value.

| Upgrade category | Immediate feel | Long-term value | Priority weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curse mitigation / resistance | Low | Very high | S |
| Curse conversion (tatari into power) | Medium | Very high | S |
| Purge and cleanse tools | High | High | A |
| Resource generation (currency, cooldowns) | Low | High | A |
| Raw damage or attack scaling | Very high | Medium | B |
| Mobility and repositioning | Medium | Medium | B |
| Cosmetic or flavor unlocks | None | None | D |

The pattern is consistent: the upgrades that feel weakest in the first hour are usually the ones that carry the build. Mitigation and conversion don't show up in a damage number, so they get skipped — and that's exactly why so many players hit a wall.

A useful habit is the three-question test before any purchase: Does this change how tatari behaves? Does it scale with the curse rather than against it? Will I still want it in ten hours? Two yeses means buy. Zero means walk away.

## The Core Tatari Upgrade Priority Tiers

Here is the ranking framework that most experienced players converge on, regardless of which specific tatari system they're working with. Player experience across multiple curse-based progression games tends to land in the same order.

| Tier | What belongs here | Why it ranks here | When to break the rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | Curse mitigation, curse conversion | Changes the rules of the mechanic | Almost never |
| A | Purge tools, resource generation | Removes dead ends, funds everything else | If you're already capped on mitigation |
| B | Damage scaling, mobility | Multiplies what you already have | Once S and A are solid |
| C | Situational utility, niche resistances | Helpful in specific fights only | When a specific encounter demands it |
| D | Cosmetics, flavor perks | No mechanical impact | After everything else is done |

The reason conversion sits at the top is simple math. Mitigation reduces what the curse takes from you. Conversion turns what the curse takes into something you can spend. Together they flip the system from a tax into an engine, and every later upgrade gets cheaper in practice because you're no longer spending resources patching damage.

If your build genuinely cannot access conversion — some tatari trees gate it behind story progress — then mitigation and purge tools become your S tier by default. Don't force a conversion build you can't reach yet.

## Stage-by-Stage Priority: Early, Mid, and Late

The right tatari upgrade priority changes as your resource income and curse thresholds shift. A tier list alone won't tell you when to buy what.

| Stage | First priority | Second priority | Hold off on | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early (first hours) | Basic mitigation | Cheap resource generation | Damage scaling | You need survival and income before multipliers |
| Mid (build defining) | Conversion or purge | Cooldown reduction | Cosmetics, niche utility | This is where the curse becomes an engine |
| Late (optimization) | Damage scaling on top of S tier | Mobility, utility | More mitigation past cap | Extra mitigation past the cap is wasted points |
| Endgame / repeat runs | Respec toward speed | Conversion stacking | Defensive padding | Efficiency replaces safety once you know the fights |

The most common mistake in the early game is buying damage because it's the only upgrade that produces a visible change. It feels productive. It isn't, because early damage numbers don't clear the thresholds that actually gate progress.

In the mid game, the opposite error appears: players keep buying mitigation long after they've hit the cap, effectively spending currency on nothing. Check your caps before every purchase.

## Build-Specific Priority Adjustments

Two players can run the same tatari system and need completely different upgrade orders. Build archetype matters more than the tier list once you're past the early game.

| Build archetype | First three upgrades | Signature pick | Trap to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curse-stacking aggressor | Mitigation → conversion → damage | Conversion multiplier | Dumping everything into raw attack |
| Sustain / tank | Mitigation → purge → resource gen | Threshold extension | Ignoring damage entirely and stalling |
| Support / utility | Resource gen → cooldown → purge | Team cleanse | Over-investing in personal damage |
| Hybrid | Mitigation → conversion → mobility | Flexible slot | Spreading points too thin to hit any cap |

Community reports consistently flag the hybrid build as the hardest to get right, because it tempts you into buying one of everything. A hybrid only works when it commits fully to the S tier first and treats everything else as a bonus.

If you're unsure which archetype fits, default to the sustain path. It's slower, but it survives long enough to let you learn the curse timings, and those timings are what actually determine whether your later upgrades pay off.

## What to Skip, and When to Break the Rules

Skipping is a skill. Every point you don't spend on a trap is a point available for an S-tier upgrade later, and respec costs are rarely free.

- **Cosmetic and flavor perks.** Buy these last, always. They change nothing about how the curse behaves.
- **Niche resistances before you meet the niche.** A resistance upgrade for a damage type you haven't encountered is dead currency.
- **Duplicate mitigation past the cap.** Stacking past the threshold does nothing except waste a slot.
- **Damage scaling before conversion.** Multiplying a number the curse is still eating is a losing trade.

| Common trap | Why it feels right | What it actually costs |
|---|---|---|
| First upgrade into damage | Visible progress | Delays the mitigation you need to survive |
| Ignoring resource generation | It's not exciting | Slower income for the whole run |
| Buying every utility option | Feels flexible | No cap reached, weak everywhere |
| Hoarding currency for a "perfect" buy | Feels disciplined | Missed power spikes at stage transitions |

The one legitimate reason to break priority order is a hard gate: a specific boss, puzzle, or story checkpoint that requires a particular tool. Buy that tool, clear the gate, then return to the framework. Gates are exceptions, not a new plan.

## Mistakes Players Report Most Often

Community reports from curse-based progression games keep surfacing the same handful of errors, and they're all fixable with a bit of planning.

The biggest is treating the tatari upgrade priority list as a shopping list rather than a sequence. Knowing that conversion is S tier doesn't help if you buy it before you have the mitigation to survive long enough to use it. Order matters as much as tier.

The second is failing to respec. Once a run is solved, the optimal build usually shifts toward speed and efficiency, and carrying defensive padding into a solved fight just slows you down. If respecs are cheap in your system, use them.

The third is ignoring the numbers behind the curse. Thresholds, stack decay, and conversion rates are usually visible somewhere in the UI. Players who track them make better upgrade decisions than players who go on feel — every time.

## FAQ

**What is the single most important tatari upgrade priority for a new player?**
Mitigation first, every time. Until you can survive the curse long enough to learn its timing, no other upgrade will have a chance to matter. Once survival is stable, move immediately into conversion or purge tools.

**Should I ever buy damage first in a tatari build?**
Only if you're replaying content you've already solved and know exactly where the thresholds sit. On a first run, early damage scaling delays the upgrades that actually gate your progress, and the curse will punish that delay.

**How often should I revisit my tatari upgrade priority?**
At every stage transition — early to mid, mid to late, and again when you start repeat runs. Caps, income, and enemy behavior all shift, and a priority order that was correct an hour ago can quietly become wasteful.

**Does tatari upgrade priority change in co-op or multiplayer?**
Yes. In group play, the priority shifts toward team-wide purge tools and shared resource generation, because one player covering the group's curse management frees everyone else to invest in damage. Coordinate before you spend.

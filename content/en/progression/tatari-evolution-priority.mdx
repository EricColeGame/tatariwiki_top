## What Tatari Are — and Why Evolution Order Decides Your Run

Tatari are the Japanese spirits born from unresolved grievance — wronged, forgotten, or unappeased — and in folklore-inspired games, they grow. Choosing the right **tatari evolution priority** early decides whether your first ten hours snowball into a comfortable mid-game or grind to a halt. This guide breaks down how **tatari evolution priority** actually works, which stages are worth chasing, and how to rank upgrades for the way you play.

In Japanese folk belief, tatari was never something you simply destroyed. The traditional response was to recognize the spirit, honor it, and redirect its power — a practice tied to the veneration of vengeful spirits in Shinto tradition. You can read more about the broader religious context through [Britannica's overview of Shinto beliefs](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Shinto).

That cultural logic maps cleanly onto progression design. Tatari-type units almost always start weak, vague, and cheap to maintain, then escalate in stages as you feed them the right resources. Each evolution unlocks a new ability tier, but each one also raises upkeep, unlocks harder content gates, or locks you out of an alternate path.

The result is a bottleneck. You cannot max everything, and spreading resources evenly is the single most common way players stall out. Evolution is a commitment, not a checkbox.

## The Three-Stage Tatari Escalation Model

Most tatari progression follows a recognizable arc borrowed from the folklore itself: a private grievance becomes a household problem, which becomes a regional calamity. Recognizing which stage you are in tells you whether to invest or wait.

| Stage | Folklore Parallel | In-Game Signs | Cost to Advance | Should You Evolve Now? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Latent | A grudge nobody remembers | Low stats, cheap upkeep, few abilities | Low | Yes — almost always |
| Manifest | A household plagued by misfortune | Mid-tier abilities, rising upkeep, first synergy unlocks | Medium | Yes, if it fits your core team |
| Rampant | A province-level calamity requiring appeasement | High stats, high upkeep, exclusive ability paths | High | Only with a clear plan |
| Appeased | The spirit honored and redirected | Utility form, support or debuff role | Respec cost | Situational — depends on build |

The key insight is that the first evolution is almost never a mistake. Early tiers are cheap, reversible in spirit, and unlock the toolset you need to evaluate later stages. The third and fourth tiers are where a bad **tatari evolution priority** call can cost you dozens of hours.

Community reports from players running curse-focused teams consistently describe the same pattern: the jump from Manifest to Rampant is the single biggest power spike and the single biggest resource trap.

## The Core Rule: Rank by Bottleneck, Not by Power

New players rank evolutions by raw stat gain. Experienced players rank them by what the evolution *unblocks*. A slightly weaker form that opens a resource node, a team synergy, or a damage-type answer is worth more than a stronger form that does nothing new.

Ask three questions before every evolution:

- **What does this unlock?** A new ability, a new zone, a new synergy, or nothing but numbers?
- **What does it cost me going forward?** Upkeep, opportunity cost, and the alternate path you are giving up.
- **Is it reversible?** If respec is cheap, be aggressive. If it is permanent, be patient.

| Decision Signal | Evolve Immediately | Wait or Skip |
|---|---|---|
| Unlocks a new damage type | Yes | — |
| Unlocks a resource or farming node | Yes | — |
| Pure stat increase, no new ability | — | Wait for a discount or event |
| Requires a rare, single-use material | — | Only if it is your core carry |
| Locks out an alternate evolution path | — | Decide your endgame build first |
| Raises upkeep beyond your income | — | Fix economy before evolving |

This framework matters more than any tier list, because tier lists assume a fully built roster. You do not have one yet.

## Matching Your Tatari Evolution Priority to Your Playstyle

Player experience varies enormously here, and the "correct" order shifts depending on how you actually play.

| Playstyle | First Priority | Second Priority | Avoid Early |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aggressive raider | Carry damage tier 1 and 2 | Control tatari tier 1 | Defensive sustain |
| Control and attrition | Debuff tatari tier 1 and 2 | Economy unlock | Glass-cannon carries |
| Support and team play | Synergy-enabling tatari | Carry damage tier 1 | Solo-carry scaling |
| Completionist | Any evolution that unlocks a new entry or zone | Carry damage tier 1 | Third-tier bench evolutions |
| Low-time / casual | Economy unlock | Carry damage tier 1 | Anything with high upkeep |

If you only take one thing from this section: match your evolutions to the content you actually run. Evolving a raid-focused tatari when you only play solo story content is a waste, no matter how high it sits on a community tier list.

## Five Mistakes That Wreck Your Tatari Evolution Priority

Most stalled runs come down to a handful of repeatable errors.

- **Spreading materials across every tatari.** You end up with six mediocre units and no carry. Concentrate first, diversify later.
- **Evolving for stats instead of unlocks.** A raw stat bump rarely changes what content you can clear. A new ability often does.
- **Ignoring upkeep.** Higher-tier tatari often demand ongoing resources. If your income cannot support them, the evolution is a downgrade in practice.
- **Committing to a permanent path before knowing your endgame build.** Permanent choices deserve patience.
- **Following a tier list written for a different patch or roster.** Meta shifts; your roster is unique.

A simple habit fixes most of this: before any evolution, write down in one sentence what it unlocks. If you cannot finish the sentence, wait.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Tatari Evolution Priority

**Which tatari should I evolve first?**

Your primary damage dealer's first two tiers, nearly every time. That evolution is the cheapest meaningful power spike available and it carries you through early and mid content. After that, prioritize any evolution that unlocks a farming or resource node so your later choices get cheaper.

**Is it ever correct to evolve a support tatari before a carry?**

Yes, in two situations: when the support evolution enables a synergy that multiplies your whole team's output, or when your carry is already strong enough for the content you are running. Outside those cases, carry first remains the safer **tatari evolution priority**.

**Should I ever skip an evolution entirely?**

Regularly. Pure stat upgrades with no new ability, third-tier evolutions on units you never field, and any evolution that raises upkeep beyond your income are all reasonable skips. Skipping is a strategy, not a failure.

**How do I know if my evolution order is working?**

Track how quickly you clear the next content gate after each evolution. If a new evolution does not measurably speed up your clears or open new content within a few sessions, your priority was probably misaligned — adjust before investing further.

## The Bottom Line

Tatari progression rewards patience and punishes novelty-chasing. Push your core carry, unlock your economy, then broaden into control and utility as your roster demands it. Keep a written record of what each evolution unlocks, respect your upkeep ceiling, and treat permanent paths as long-term commitments rather than impulse purchases.

Do that, and your tatari evolution priority stops being a guess and starts being a plan — one that scales from your first Manifest spirit all the way to the endgame.

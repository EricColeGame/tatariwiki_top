## Why Tatari Evolution Decisions Shape Your Whole Run

Curse-bound spirits look like filler early on. They sit at the back of your roster, soak up experience, and occasionally land a debuff. Then the difficulty curve arrives, and the tatari you invested in becomes the only thing holding your formation together.

That is why "tatari who to evolve" is one of the most-asked progression questions in folklore-inspired creature games. Evolution is rarely cosmetic: it usually rewrites a stat spread, unlocks an ability slot, and changes how a spirit interacts with status effects. Get it right and a mid-tier unit carries you into the late game. Get it wrong and you spend hours re-farming materials you already burned.

Tatari draw on a long tradition of Japanese curse folklore — the idea that a grudge, once fed, becomes something with its own will. You can read more about [the yōkai tradition that shaped these creatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C5%8Dkai) if you want the cultural background. Games borrow that idea and turn it into a progression hook: the longer a tatari is neglected, the more it costs to bring online, but the more dramatic its evolved form tends to be.

## What Actually Changes When a Tatari Evolves

Before you commit a single material, understand what an evolution does to the unit. In most games built around curse-bound spirits, four things change at once — and only one of them is visible on the preview screen.

| Change | What it affects | What to verify first |
|---|---|---|
| Stat redistribution | Bulk, speed, or attack power shifts toward a new role | Check whether the evolved form keeps the role you actually need |
| Ability unlock | Adds a passive or active skill | Confirm the skill scales off the stat your build already invests in |
| Cost curve | Feeding, fusion, and material requirements rise | Price the full path before evolving, not just the first step |
| Reversal rules | Some titles allow devolution, most do not | Treat every evolution as permanent unless the game says otherwise |

The trap is the ability unlock. A flashy new skill tempts players into evolving a tatari that was performing fine in its base form, only to discover the new skill scales off a stat they never trained. Preview screens usually show base stats, not growth curves — so a tatari that looks identical to its base form on paper may end up with a completely different stat ceiling twenty levels later.

## Tatari Who to Evolve: A Priority Framework

When players ask tatari who to evolve, they usually want a ranking. Rankings go stale fast across patches and playstyles, so use a priority framework instead. It holds up whether you are running a curse-stacking build, a tanky attrition team, or a speed-focused burst lineup.

| Tier | Profile | Evolve when | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| S — Fixes a hole | The evolved form covers a damage type or role your team lacks | Immediately, as soon as materials allow | Low; the unit earns a permanent slot |
| A — Scales hard | Base form is mediocre, evolved form has strong growth | Once your core three are stable | Medium; delays other investments |
| B — Situational | Strong only against specific enemy types or bosses | After you clear the current difficulty wall | Medium; may be obsolete next patch |
| C — Sidegrade | Evolved form trades one strength for another | Only for collection or challenge runs | High; usually a net loss for progression |

The framework forces one honest question: does this evolution solve a problem I actually have? If the answer is no, the tatari stays in the jar. That single filter saves more materials than any tier list.

### Read Your Roster Before You Commit

- **Coverage:** List the damage types and roles your current team handles. Evolve into the gap, not into a duplicate.
- **Speed tiers:** A slow evolved form that hits hard is useless if it never gets a turn before the enemy's opener.
- **Ability synergy:** Check whether your supports can enable the evolved form's new skill. A curse-amplifier passive needs a curse-applier standing next to it.
- **Material budget:** Count the full cost. Many players stall halfway because the final stage needs a rare drop they already spent elsewhere.

## Evolution Timing: Level, Materials, and Patience

Answering tatari who to evolve is only half the job — timing is the other half. Evolving too early can lock you out of a base-form skill; evolving too late wastes experience that could have gone into a stronger stat curve.

| Timing strategy | When it works | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Evolve ASAP | The evolved form learns everything the base form did | You may miss a base-form-only utility skill |
| Wait for a skill milestone | The base form has a key move at a specific level | Slower power spike; more grinding |
| Wait for materials | Rare evolution items are scarce | Stronger, cheaper run later, but a weak mid-game |
| Batch evolutions | You are prepping for a known difficulty spike | Requires planning two zones ahead |

A practical rule: if the base form still learns skills, check the list before evolving. If nothing useful remains, evolve immediately — there is no reason to delay. If a key move is still two levels away, wait for it.

Community reports from long-running progression threads consistently favor batching evolutions before a known boss wall rather than spreading them out. Spreading them thin leaves you with five mediocre units instead of one carry. The exception is a tatari that unlocks a defensive passive early — those are worth evolving on the spot because they smooth out the entire mid-game.

## How to Test a Candidate Before You Commit

You do not have to gamble. Most progression systems give you enough information to test a tatari without spending rare materials.

| Test | How to run it | What a pass looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Field trial | Take the base form into three fights at the current difficulty | It survives long enough to act twice |
| Role check | Swap it into the slot you want the evolved form to fill | The team does not lose coverage |
| Cost audit | Total every material in the evolution chain | You can afford the final stage today |
| Skill review | Compare base and evolved skill lists side by side | No unique base-form utility is lost |

If a candidate fails two of these four tests, bench it. There is no shortage of tatari, and there is a real shortage of evolution items.

## Common Mistakes That Waste Materials

- Evolving the highest-level tatari instead of the most useful one.
- Ignoring the evolved form's new weaknesses, especially a shift from physical to special defense.
- Spending rare evolution items on a C-tier sidegrade because the art looked good.
- Forgetting that some tatari require a bond, trust, or affinity threshold before evolution unlocks.
- Evolving a support tatari into an attacker and then discovering your team has no healer left.

Each of these mistakes is cheap to avoid and expensive to fix. Ten minutes of roster review beats ten hours of re-farming, and the review costs nothing but attention.

| Step | Action | Output |

Run this checklist every time you clear a major zone. Revisit the tatari who to evolve question then, not in the middle of a fight when you are reacting instead of planning.

## Budgeting: Where the Real Cost Hides

Most tatari who to evolve debates focus on stats and ignore the economy. That is backwards. In almost every progression system, the bottleneck is not the creature — it is the material pipeline behind it.

| Resource | Typical pressure point | How to ease it |
|---|---|---|
| Evolution items | Rare drops from specific enemy types | Farm during story missions, not after |
| Currency | Costs spike sharply at the final stage | Keep a reserve equal to one full evolution |
| Experience fodder | Feeding costs rise with each stage | Level the base form before evolving |
| Time-gated items | Daily or weekly lockouts | Start the timer before you need the unit |

Player experience suggests that keeping one full evolution's worth of resources in reserve is the single best habit for avoiding mid-run stalls. It also makes the "evolve now or wait" decision much less stressful, because you are choosing between two good options rather than gambling your entire stockpile.

## Final Thoughts

There is no universal ranking of tatari. There is only your roster, your current wall, and your material stock. Answer the tatari who to evolve question by asking which unit removes a problem you are actually stuck on — then commit fully rather than hedging across three half-built spirits.

Evolve for coverage, time it around skill milestones, and budget for the final stage before you start. Do that, and your curse-bound spirits stop being benchwarmers and start being the reason your run survives.

## FAQ

**Is it ever worth evolving a B-tier tatari?**
Yes — when it counters the specific boss or zone you are stuck on. A situational tatari that clears a wall is worth more than a tier-list favorite that does nothing for your current matchup. That is the practical way to answer tatari who to evolve for your own save file.

**Should I evolve my tatari as soon as it hits the required level?**
Only if the base form has no useful skills left to learn. Check the skill list first; if a key utility move unlocks later, wait for it and evolve afterward.

**Can I undo an evolution?**
Some games allow devolution, but many do not. Assume permanence and verify the rules in your specific title before committing rare materials to a sidegrade.

**Do I need to evolve every tatari I own?**
No. Most rosters only need two or three fully evolved curse-bound spirits. The rest are better left as base forms, utility units, or bonding partners for the ones you actually field.

## What Makes the Tatari Horde Invasion Different

Few modes punish hesitation faster than the tatari horde invasion. Cursed spirits pour into the arena in overlapping waves, and every second spent debating a target is a second the swarm uses to close the gap. That pressure is the point: a tatari horde invasion strips a build down to fundamentals — spacing, target priority, and resource discipline — and rewards players who plan before the first spawn.

The name is borrowed from Japanese folklore, where *tatari* refers to a curse laid by a wronged or restless spirit. The mode leans into that idea. Enemies are not a standing army waiting politely at a gate; they are an accumulated grudge that keeps arriving, and the arena slowly becomes the thing you are fighting as much as the enemies in it.

| Trait | Tatari Horde Invasion | Standard survival | Boss rush |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spawn flow | Layered waves with short breathers | Steady trickle | One encounter at a time |
| Difficulty curve | Steep, steps up every few waves | Mostly flat | Spikes per boss |
| Resource economy | Tight; pickups land between waves | Generous | Reset each fight |
| Failure state | Squad wipe ends the run | Respawn on a timer | Checkpoint retry |
| Core skill tested | Target priority and spacing | Endurance | Pattern recognition |
| Typical session | Short to medium | Long | Fixed length |

If you already enjoy wave-based design, the [horde survival category on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/tags/en/Horde%20Survival/) shows how widely this language is used across games. The tatari variant stands out because it front-loads ranged threats instead of saving them for the late waves.

## How a Tatari Horde Invasion Run Unfolds

Most builds of the mode follow the same broad rhythm: a gentle opening, a sudden jump in pressure, an elite checkpoint, a named boss, and then an endless overtime phase that exists purely to end your run. Community-reported wave bands vary by difficulty and patch, so treat the ranges below as a shape rather than a fixed script.

| Phase | Typical waves | What spawns | Your job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | 1–3 | Scattered lesser tatari | Learn spawn lanes, bank cooldowns |
| Pressure | 4–8 | Mixed melee and ranged | Kill ranged units first, hold a corner |
| Elite | 9–12 | Armored elites, ward/healer units | Burst healers before they stack |
| Boss | 13–15 | Named tatari with arena changes | Split aggro, burn during recovery frames |
| Overtime | 16+ | Everything, faster, more of it | Survive, bank rewards, reset your best |

### Positioning rules that hold up in every wave

- **Never fight in the open.** A doorway, a stairwell, or a corner turns six attackers into two.
- **Rotate early, not late.** The moment your exit lane fills with bodies, you have already lost the position.
- **Track the timer, not the kill feed.** Breathers between waves are for reloading, healing, and repositioning — not for chasing stragglers.
- **Leave one escape route unblocked.** Area damage builds especially tend to wall themselves in without noticing.

## Tatari Enemy Types and Target Priority

Target priority is the single biggest skill gap between players who reach the boss and players who stall around wave six. The rule of thumb: anything that multiplies the enemy's effective health or damage dies first, regardless of how scary the big thing in front of you looks.

| Enemy | Tell | Real threat | Counter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lesser tatari | Slow shamble, low health | Chip damage, blocks your shots | Area damage, keep moving |
| Whisperer (ranged) | Hovers, glows before firing | Punishes stationary play | Close distance or break line of sight |
| Ward keeper | Pulses, links to nearby enemies | Extends every fight it touches | Focus it down, ignore the adds |
| Elite brute | Armor plates, telegraphed charge | One-shots squishy builds | Sidestep the charge, hit flanks |
| Named boss | Unique intro, arena shift | Wipes unprepared squads | Kite in a loop, punish recovery windows |

Player experience suggests the ward keeper is the most underrated enemy in the mode. Squads that ignore it often blame the boss for a wipe that actually started three waves earlier, when healing pulses quietly undid every bit of damage the team dealt.

## Loadouts and Team Roles That Survive Longer

Solo players have to cover every role at once, which is why solo runs usually end earlier than coordinated group runs. In a squad, four players who each commit hard to one job will almost always outperform four generalists.

| Role | Job | Stats to prioritize | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crowd clearer | Thin the swarm, protect lanes | Area damage, reload speed | Pushing past the team's front line |
| Single-target DPS | Delete elites and bosses | Burst damage, crit chance | Ignoring adds to tunnel the boss |
| Controller | Slow, root, and funnel enemies | Cooldown reduction, duration | Wasting crowd control on grunts |
| Support | Heal, revive, restock ammo | Sustain, mobility | Standing too far back to reach anyone |

A few build principles apply almost everywhere in the tatari horde invasion:

- **Bring one answer for crowds and one for armor.** Pure single-target builds stall on wave five; pure area builds stall on the first elite.
- **Value cooldown reduction over raw damage.** Getting an escape or crowd-control tool back ten seconds sooner saves more runs than a marginal damage bump.
- **Pack mobility.** Every wipe in a horde mode is ultimately a movement failure.
- **Test your build in the opening waves.** If waves one through three feel tight, the run is already over.

## Rewards, Difficulty Scaling, and Progression

Reward structures differ between builds of the mode, but the underlying shape is consistent: payouts scale with wave count, and the value per minute peaks around boss clears rather than at the deepest possible wave. Grinding overtime waves is usually the least efficient way to earn currency, even though it feels the most impressive.

| Milestone | Typical reward | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| First clear | Core currency, mode unlock | One-time bonus |
| Every few waves | Escalating currency drops | Diminishing returns per minute |
| Boss defeat | Gear or blueprint chance | Best value per minute |
| New personal best | Cosmetic or title | Cosmetic only |

Difficulty scaling in most builds raises enemy health and spawn density faster than it raises your own power, which is why the mode eventually ends for everyone. The practical takeaway: set a target wave, hit it, extract, and spend the rewards rather than chasing one more round with a broken build.

If you want background on the folklore the mode draws from, the [goryō vengeful spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gory%C5%8D) article is a solid starting point for understanding why "tatari" carries the connotation of a curse that cannot simply be killed once.

## FAQ

**How long does a tatari horde invasion run usually last?**
Most runs land somewhere between five and twenty minutes. The opening waves move quickly; the length is decided by how long your squad survives the elite and boss phases. Community reports suggest that once you pass the boss, overtime tends to end things fast.

**Is the tatari horde invasion mode better solo or with a squad?**
Squads go deeper, because four players can each specialize instead of splitting attention across crowd control, single-target damage, and support. Solo play is still worth doing to learn spawn patterns and practice your movement, but expect a lower wave ceiling.

**What is the most common reason squads wipe?**
Tunnel vision. Teams fixate on the boss or the biggest elite while a ward keeper heals everything on screen, or they hold a position two seconds past the point where it was defensible. Fixing target priority and rotating earlier solves more wipes than any build change.

**Do rewards scale with difficulty?**
Yes, higher difficulty settings generally raise both currency and gear quality, but they also shorten your runs. If you are farming rather than pushing a personal best, the sweet spot is usually the highest difficulty you can clear consistently without a wipe.

**Do I need to learn the folklore to enjoy the mode?**
Not at all. It adds flavor to the enemy design, but the mechanics stand on their own. Treat the tatari as what they are in the arena: a wave that never fully stops coming.
